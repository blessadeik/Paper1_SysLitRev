**What the review is about**

Some people lose the ability to speak but keep their thinking intact, for example after ALS (motor neurone disease), a brainstem stroke or severe paralysis. Researchers are building "brain-to-speech" systems that record brain activity and use artificial intelligence (AI) to turn it into text or synthesised speech. Signals are recorded with implanted electrodes, or non-invasively with EEG caps, MEG or fMRI scanners.

Our systematic review asks three questions about the research published between January 2015 and April 2026:
1. **Methods:** which AI methods are used to decode speech from brain signals?
2. **Performance:** how well do they work, and can results from different studies fairly be compared?
3. **Generalisation:** does a system trained on one person, session or language still work for another?

We plan to submit the paper to *Nature Machine Intelligence*.

**Why a second, independent reviewer matters**

A systematic review must show that the choice of studies did not depend on one person's judgement. That is why two people screen every record independently, without seeing each other's decisions. We then measure how often we agreed (a statistic called Cohen's kappa) and resolve any disagreements together. The method is registered in advance on PROSPERO, the international register of systematic reviews (record CRD420261368521), and you are being added to that record as a member of the review team.

You do not need to be an expert in AI or neuroscience. Each decision is about whether a paper meets a short list of criteria, and the workbook explains them.

6. **Writing and approval.** You read the manuscript drafts, comment and suggest edits, and approve the final version before submission. As a co-author, you share responsibility for the paper's accuracy.

**Rules for screening (important)**

- **Work independently.** Do not discuss individual decisions with me or anyone else until we have both finished each stage. Please do not send me your file early.
- **Do not use AI tools** (ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, etc.) to make or check screening decisions. The decisions must be yours.
- **Decide from the title and abstract only** at the first stage. Do not look up the full paper.
- **When in doubt, choose Unsure.** Unsure is not a failure: it sends the paper to full-text review, which is where doubtful cases should be settled. Excluding a relevant paper by mistake is worse than keeping an irrelevant one for a second look.
- **Choose the first exclusion reason that applies,** and use the Notes column for anything you want to raise later.
- **Write down the dates.** Record your name and the dates you started and finished on the *Progress* tab.

**A few examples of how the criteria work**

- An implanted-electrode study that decodes attempted words in a person with ALS and reports accuracy: **Include**.
- A study decoding hand or arm movements, not speech: **Exclude, E3**.
- A study in monkeys or rats: **Exclude, E1**.
- A study recording facial muscles with surface EMG, not the brain: **Exclude, E2**.
- A review article summarising other studies: **Exclude, E6**.
- An EEG speech study whose abstract does not say whether a decoding model was used: **Unsure**.
